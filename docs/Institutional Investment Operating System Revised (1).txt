## Portfolio Feedback Risk Engine.

This is exactly why your AI investment system should not simply calculate:

"Intrinsic value = $X."

It also needs to calculate:
"What happens if the market moves against us?"


## 1. Fundamental & Valuation Risk

* What is the company actually worth?
* Is the investment thesis deteriorating?
* Has the security become too expensive?
* What happens if the thesis reverses?

## 2. Position & Concentration Risk

* How much capital is exposed to the position?
* What happens if the position falls 10%, 20%, 30%, or 40%?
* How much of the portfolio depends on the same thesis?
* Are apparently different positions actually the same macro trade?

## 3. Factor & Correlation Risk

* What macro, industry, or factor exposure drives the position?
* What other positions move with it?
* What happens if correlated positions fall simultaneously?

## 4. Leverage & Margin Risk

* How much borrowed capital supports the position?
* How much will leverage magnify losses?
* At what point do margin requirements or lenders force action?
* What happens if the broker increases margin requirements during a crisis?

## 5. Liquidity & Financing Risk

* How quickly can we exit without materially affecting the price?
* Can the portfolio survive a severe drawdown without forced liquidation?
* Can we continue financing the position long enough for the thesis to play out?

## 6. Volatility & Market-Structure Risk

* What happens when volatility doubles?
* How rapidly is risk increasing?
* Could options, dealer hedging, or crowded positioning amplify the move?
* How many other investors are positioned similarly?

## 7. Feedback & Path-Dependency Risk

* Could our own selling contribute to the decline?
* Could falling prices trigger further selling or liquidation?
* What happens if the asset eventually recovers, but we are forced out before the recovery?

### Simplified Risk Framework

**1. Fundamental Risk** — Is the thesis and valuation still valid?
**2. Position Risk** — How much can this individual position hurt us?
**3. Concentration Risk** — How much of the portfolio depends on the same outcome?
**4. Leverage Risk** — How much can borrowing magnify the loss?
**5. Liquidity Risk** — Can we exit or survive the drawdown?
**6. Market-Structure Risk** — Could volatility, crowding, or derivatives amplify the move?
**7. Path Risk** — Can we survive the journey until the thesis plays out?
